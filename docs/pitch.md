# 3-minute pitch (draft)

**1. Hook (20 s)**
"In February 2023, Cyclone Gabrielle hit Hawke's Bay. Farmers had hours to decide where to move their animals, in the dark, with the power out."
(Keep it respectful. No dramatic images.)

**2. Problem (20 s)**
"Warnings come by region. But decisions happen paddock by paddock. At 5am a farmer is piecing it together from the MetService app, the council river page and what they remember from the last flood."

**3. Demo (90 s)**
- Sample farm on the Hawke's Bay plains.
- Press **Replay warning** → "This is a saved MetService warning, replayed."
- Paddocks by the river turn red → banner: "Move the herd to Paddock 7 by [time shown]." (Replay sets onset 7 hours from now, so the deadline is 4 hours from now.)
- Point at Paddock 1 → "1 m above the river, about 300 m away, inside a red warning."
- **Show plan** → moves, milking forward, generator, water, neighbours, Civil Defence → print/share.

**4. How it works (30 s)**
"Real New Zealand data: MetService warnings, LINZ elevation, Hawke's Bay Regional Council river gauges. Simple, explainable scoring. Gemini turns it into a plan a tired person can follow, and it's not allowed to invent facts."

**5. Real user (10 s)**
"[Farmer quote, with permission]."

**6. What's next (10 s)**
"Text alerts when a warning covers your farm, more regions, LiDAR-level detail, and working with councils and Civil Defence."

**Close:** "HighGround: know where your herd should be before the river rises."

## Likely judge questions

- *How accurate is it?* — It's guidance from public data with simple, visible rules; official advice comes first. Accuracy improves with LiDAR and local river gauges.
- *Why not just use the MetService app?* — It tells you the region is at risk; HighGround tells you which paddock and by when.
- *What did Gemini do?* — It writes the plan from facts we computed; the safety-critical numbers come from code, not the model.
