# Farmer interview (10 minutes)

A short, friendly chat with one farmer or rural contact about storm decisions and moving stock. We want to learn, and, only with permission, to quote them in the pitch.

## Before you start

- Say who you are and what HighGround is in one sentence: "We're building a hackathon app that turns weather warnings into a plan for which paddocks to move stock out of."
- Say how long it takes (about 10 minutes) and that they can skip any question.
- If talking about Cyclone Gabrielle or another flood, let them lead. Don't push for details of losses.

## Questions

1. When a heavy-rain or red warning comes out, how do you usually hear about it first?
2. What do you look at to decide whether to move stock? (Warnings, river levels, the sky, neighbours, past floods?)
3. How do you know which paddocks are the risky ones?
4. How much warning do you need to move a mob safely, and what slows you down?
5. Who else on the farm needs to know the plan, and how do you tell them now?
6. Thinking about the last big storm, what would have helped you decide sooner?
7. If an app said "Move the herd to Paddock 7 by 4pm", would you trust it? What would it need to show you?
8. Anything we haven't asked that we should know?
